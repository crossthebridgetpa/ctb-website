Before Call One

A little prep goes a long way

No slides and no documents. The more real the workflow you show us, the sharper the report gets.

Every number in the report traces back to your answers here, so rough guesses beat blanks.

  • Pick one workflow that eats your week. Be ready to run it live on a screen share. Show the messy version, not the tidy one.
  • Know your goal in a sentence. More revenue, more free time, or fewer mistakes. Pick the one that matters most.
  • List what you repeat and what you dread. Then name the one thing you'd fix with a magic wand.
  • Put a number on an hour. Yours, and whoever does the work. The savings math in the report starts here.
  • Note what you've already tried. Include the AI tools you pay for, and the ones you quit.
  • Jot down the ten tools you use most. Email, calendar, CRM, spreadsheets, whatever runs the day.
  • Block 45 quiet minutes at a laptop. Open the tools you plan to show, and close anything with customer data you'd rather not share.
On Call One

How the working interview feels

We interrupt a lot

We ask what you physically did next, not what the system is supposed to do. That's how we find the slow, repetitive part.

We hold the answers for call two

Recommending on the spot is how businesses end up with a clever tool aimed at the wrong problem. Call one is for understanding.

You choose what to show

We don't need logins or customer data. In regulated industries we stay on back-office work and never touch client data.

Bring whoever does the work

If someone on your team runs the workflow day to day, invite them. You attend both calls, because the plan is yours to approve.
