Why It Matters

Why does it matter whether AI can read my website?

When someone asks Meta AI, ChatGPT or Claude which local business to call, the assistant answers from what it can read on the web. If your site hides its facts in images, scripts or vague slogans, the assistant has little to work with, and it is more likely to name a competitor whose site it can read.

Most fixes are small: plain text instead of pictures, clear labels, and a straight answer to the question your customers ask.

Can the AI crawlers get in? Some website settings block them without the owner knowing.
Can they read the important parts? Prices, services and reviews shown as images or loaded by scripts are invisible to them.
Does the page say plainly what you do? A slogan tells an assistant nothing. One clear sentence does.
Is it current? Dated facts tell an assistant the information is fresh.
What We Check

Eleven checks, in six groups

We read your homepage and one or two pages that sell, the way an AI crawler does: raw text, no scripts. The checklist reflects how AI assistants read websites as of October 2026.

1

Crawler access

The settings that let Meta AI, ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity read your site, or quietly block them.

2

Text, not pictures

Prices, services and reviews as real text. Anything inside an image or loaded by a script is invisible to an AI crawler.

3

Labels a machine can read

Named buttons, labeled form fields, image descriptions and a standard page structure.

4

The question your customers ask

Your buyer's main question as a heading, with a plain answer in under 100 words.

5

A definition, not a slogan

One sentence that says what you are, who you serve and what you do.

6

Fresh, working pages

Dated facts, a visible updated date, and no broken links.
